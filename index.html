    Project Scope

    Literature Review

    Urban Waste Crisis

    The World Bank projects that global solid waste generation will reach 3.4 billion tons annually by 2050, a 70% increase on 2016 levels. In Sri Lanka, the Colombo Municipal Council alone generates approximately 800 tons of municipal solid waste per day, and existing infrastructure operates well below the level needed for effective management.

    IoT in Waste Management

    Low-cost IoT platforms, particularly ESP32-class microcontrollers with integrated Wi-Fi, and cloud services such as Firebase make intelligent, data-driven waste management feasible. Existing smart bins predominantly monitor a single parameter — fill level — and lack environmental and gas sensing capabilities.

    ML-Based Prediction

    Research demonstrates that machine learning models, particularly XGBoost regression, can effectively predict waste volumes using historical municipal records combined with rainfall, temporal, zonal, demographic, and calendar features. Dynamic, sensor-informed routing can reduce operational cost by up to 40% compared with static scheduling.

    Smart Collection Systems

    Existing solutions address waste collection with fixed schedules and limited real-time information. There is a need for an integrated mobile workflow connecting IoT-derived critical-bin information with resident–driver coordination, combining GPS tracking, route assistance, and proximity-based notifications.

    Identification

    Research Gap

    01

    Absence of Environmental Sensing

    Most commercial smart bins report fill level only; hazardous-gas (methane, ammonia, CO₂) and thermal-environmental data are missing from the telemetry, leaving sanitation workers exposed to avoidable gas hazards.

    Component 1
    02

    Limited Behavioral Monitoring

    Existing systems show that a bin is full but not how it got that way. There is no detection of illegal dumping, sudden fill spikes, abnormal lid activity, extended lid opening, or imminent overflow patterns.

    Component 2
    03

    Insufficient Data for ML Models

    Single-parameter fill-level data provide insufficient features for waste composition prediction. Localized municipal-level forecasting combining environmental, temporal, and demographic features is largely unexplored for Sri Lankan contexts.

    Component 3
    04

    Weak Coordination Workflows

    There is a gap in integrating IoT-derived critical-bin information with a unified mobile coordination workflow for resident–driver communication, live vehicle tracking, and proximity-based arrival notifications.

    Component 4
    Core Challenge

    Research Problem & Solution

    The Problem

    Conventional municipal waste collection follows fixed routes and schedules with no real-time knowledge of bin conditions. Waste collection decisions are made without knowledge of the environmental state of each bin — a half-full bin emitting elevated methane or ammonia may need priority collection for safety and odour reasons, yet no fill-level-only system can flag it. This leads to:

    • Premature collection of part-filled bins
    • Delayed service to overflowing bins
    • Higher fuel consumption and excess labour
    • Worker exposure to hazardous gas conditions
    • Avoidable environmental contamination

    Our Solution

    A comprehensive Smart Waste Management System that builds an end-to-end predictive framework combining four integrated components:

    • IoT-based environmental and gas sensing with real-time cloud telemetry
    • Smart fill-level monitoring with anomaly detection and behavior-aware tracking
    • ML-based municipal waste prediction, composition estimation, and circular economy revenue modeling
    • Mobile application for real-time bin detection, adaptive route optimization, and resident-driver coordination
    Goals

    Research Objectives

    Environmental Sensing Smart Bin

    Hemsara K.D.S. (IT22548528)
    • Design and build an ESP32-based sensing unit with MQ-135, MQ-4, and DHT22 sensors
    • Implement GREEN/YELLOW/RED criticality classification using multi-parameter thresholds
    • Publish timestamped JSON telemetry to Firebase Realtime Database
    • Achieve defined targets for sensor accuracy, alert accuracy, delivery rate, latency and uptime

    Smart Fill-Level & Occupancy Monitoring

    Gamaariya C.C. (IT22202772)
    • Combine HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor and magnetic reed switch on ESP32
    • Classify bin fill into OK, Warning, and Critical status levels
    • Implement rule-based anomaly detection for illegal dumping, fill spikes, and abnormal activity
    • Achieve >95% fill-classification accuracy and >90% anomaly detection accuracy

    ML-Based Waste Prediction & Revenue Hub

    Jayawardhane K.G.M.S. (IT22213358)
    • Develop XGBoost regression pipelines for wet and dry waste prediction
    • Integrate rainfall, temporal, zonal, demographic, and calendar features
    • Build composition estimation, compactor planning, and resource-recovery modules
    • Target >85% predictive accuracy on municipal test data

    Mobile App for Real-Time Bin Detection, Adaptive Route Optimization & Resident-Driven Coordination

    Wijesiri A.M. (IT22088482)
    • Integrate IoT-based smart-bin monitoring to identify bins reaching critical waste levels in real time
    • Develop a mobile map interface that enables drivers to identify and prioritize critical bins
    • Develop a resident–driver coordination mechanism for submitting and managing pickup requests
    • Implement route support for drivers based on their current location and collection points
    • Evaluate the usability, functionality, and effectiveness of the mobile-based system
    Approach

    Methodology

    The project follows a design-science, design-and-development approach: each of the four components designs and builds a working artefact and evaluates it against predefined targets. Development proceeds through problem identification, solution design, artefact creation, and evaluation.

    1

    Problem Identification

    Systematic literature review and gap analysis identifying limitations in current waste management approaches, covering IoT sensing, ML prediction, and mobile coordination.

    2

    Solution Design

    Architecture design of four integrated components with defined interfaces. Hardware selection (ESP32, MQ sensors, DHT22, HC-SR04), ML model selection (XGBoost), and mobile framework decisions.

    3

    Artifact Development

    Implementation of IoT firmware (Arduino/C++), cloud integration (Firebase Realtime Database), ML pipelines (Python/XGBoost) with a Flask back-end, web dashboards (React), and a Flutter mobile application with GPS tracking, live maps, and route support.

    4

    Evaluation & Validation

    Testing against predefined metrics: sensor accuracy, fill-status classification, anomaly detection, prediction accuracy, cloud delivery and latency, and end-to-end functional test scenarios. See Results & Findings for the measured outcomes.

    Findings

    Results & Findings

    Key evaluation results of each component, as reported in the individual theses.

    Environmental Sensing Smart Bin

    Hemsara K.D.S. (IT22548528)
    95.0% Gas-alert true-positive rate
    99.4% Payload delivery to cloud
    1.84 s Mean cloud latency
    30 days Continuous run, 2.1% missing data
    • Acquired and transmitted MQ-135, MQ-4 and DHT22 readings to Firebase in real time
    • DHT22 temperature RMSE of 0.82 °C and humidity RMSE of 2.74 %RH
    • Maximum recorded cloud latency of 4.63 s, within the 5 s target
    • Met the main predefined targets for sensing, cloud communication and long-term data collection

    Smart Fill-Level & Occupancy Monitoring

    Gamaariya C.C. (IT22202772)
    96 / 100 Fill-status test cases classified correctly
    57 / 60 Anomaly scenarios detected
    1.8 s Mean alert latency
    495 / 500 Readings received in Firebase
    • Ultrasonic distance error of 0.2–0.3 cm (MAE) at 5, 15 and 25 cm reference distances
    • Illegal dumping, fill spikes, abnormal lid activity, extended lid opening and overflow detected in 90–100% of test scenarios
    • Maximum alert latency of 2.9 s across all test runs
    • Live bin status and archived records were successfully consumed by the other components

    ML-Based Waste Prediction & Revenue Hub

    Jayawardhane K.G.M.S. (IT22213358)
    87.67% Dry-waste prediction accuracy
    80.85% Wet-waste prediction accuracy
    0.949 Dry-waste R²
    126 Held-out test records
    • Dry waste was forecast more accurately than wet waste (R² 0.9492 vs 0.8035)
    • Dry-waste R² exceeded 0.90 in all four zones
    • Mean absolute error (1.52 t dry, 3.28 t wet) stayed below the capacity of one 5-tonne truck
    • Forecasts were converted into truck trips, waste composition and revenue estimates in a working dashboard

    Mobile App for Real-Time Bin Detection, Adaptive Route Optimization & Resident-Driven Coordination

    Wijesiri A.M. (IT22088482)
    8 / 8 Workflows verified in practical tests
    3 User roles: resident, driver, admin
    ~30 Application screens
    Real-time Tracking & arrival notification
    • Resident pickup requests appeared on the driver map in real time
    • Driver movement was visible to residents, and the nearest-truck display updated as vehicles moved
    • Proximity condition triggered the expected “Truck is here” arrival notification
    • Critical IoT bin events were shown in red on the driver map
    • Registration/OTP, password recovery and admin deactivation/recovery workflows operated as expected

    Integration: The four components were integrated through Firebase. Live bin status from the IoT components was read by the mobile application, and archived sensor data was available to the ML component for time-series processing.
